Photoplay Era Rules

Principles of Scenario Construction - For the Guidance of the Department

These rules have been assembled from the teachings of photoplay-era manuals and studio practice. They are not commandments; they are hard-won wisdom. Violate them knowingly. Violate them for dramatic reasons. But know them first.

Structure
  1. Every photoplay must have a single, clearly identifiable protagonist whose desire drives every scene.
  2. The story must build in rising action; no scene may leave the audience where it found them.
  3. The climax must be visual. Emotion must be rendered in action, not dialogue.
  4. Every story must have a beginning, a middle, and an end, and each must carry proportional dramatic weight.
  5. The first scene must establish the world, the protagonist, and the central desire within minutes of screen time.
Character
  1. The protagonist must have a clear, visible desire, not merely a mood or a situation.
  2. The antagonist must be worthy of the protagonist. A weak villain makes a weak picture.
  3. Every character must want something, even if only a glass of water.
  4. Character is revealed by action under pressure, not by conversation in repose.
  5. The love story must not be an addendum. It must be essential to the dramatic spine.
  6. Women characters must have agency, intelligence, and something to lose.
Spectacle
  1. Every major production deserves one sequence of overwhelming visual power: the parting-of-the-sea moment.
  2. Spectacle without moral meaning is carnival. The set piece must reveal, punish, reward, or transform.
  3. The camera sees what the eye sees. Design scenes for the lens, not the stage.
  4. Exteriors must be used boldly. The landscape is a character. The crowd is an instrument.
  5. Night sequences have dramatic power unequalled. Use them wisely and sparingly.
Morality
  1. The photoplay must affirm the moral order, even when it depicts its violation.
  2. Sin may be shown, but its consequences must follow with inevitable dramatic logic.
  3. The happy ending is not a requirement; the earned ending is.
  4. Hypocrisy is the richest dramatic ore available to the scenario writer. Mine it without mercy.
  5. The moral question of the story must be answerable by the final scene, even if the answer is a question.
Comedy
  1. Comedy is not decoration. It is release, and without it tragedy becomes endurance.
  2. The best comedy arises from character in situation, not from jokes inserted into the action.
  3. Class contrast is an inexhaustible comic engine.
  4. The pompous character is always available to be punctured. Do not neglect this resource.
  5. Even the darkest drama requires a moment of relief, or the audience will harden against it.
Dialogue (Title Cards)
  1. Every title card must earn its presence. What can be shown must not be said.
  2. Title card dialogue must sound as though the character actually said it, not as though a writer wrote it.
  3. Exposition in title cards is a confession of failure. Find a visual way.
  4. The wittiest title cards are those that say one thing and mean another.
